# Visiting NHPT Heritage Sites

## General Access

Most National Heritage Preservation Trust sites are open to the public
during advertised hours, which vary by site and season. Some interior
spaces may have restricted access during conservation work; visitors
should check with on-site staff or the Trust's website before planning a
visit around a specific building or feature.

## Photography

Photography for personal, non-commercial use is generally permitted in
outdoor areas and most interior spaces. Flash photography and tripods may
be restricted in rooms containing sensitive painted surfaces, textiles,
or stained glass, as repeated bright light exposure can accelerate fading
over time. Commercial photography, filming, or drone use requires advance
permission from Trust staff.

## Accessibility

Many NHPT sites are historic structures with original stairs, uneven
floors, and narrow doorways that predate modern accessibility standards.
Where possible, sites provide accessible routes, and staff can advise on
the most accessible way to experience a particular building. Visitors with
specific accessibility requirements are encouraged to contact the site in
advance.

## Behaviour on Site

Visitors are asked to:
- Stay on marked routes and behind any barriers
- Avoid touching stonework, plasterwork, timber framing, or painted
  surfaces, since oils and moisture from skin contact accelerate
  long-term decay
- Keep to designated areas during any ongoing conservation work
- Report any visible damage or structural concern to staff immediately,
  rather than assuming it will be noticed

## Using This System While Visiting

This assistant can help identify the architectural style of a building or
feature from a photograph and explain what makes that style
distinctive. It is an educational and interpretive tool, not a
conservation assessment. If the system reports low confidence in its
classification, this generally means the building shows mixed influences
from more than one style, which is common in buildings that have been
altered, extended, or restored across different historical periods.
