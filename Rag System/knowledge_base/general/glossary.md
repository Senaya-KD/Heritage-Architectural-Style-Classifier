# Glossary of Architectural Terms

**Arcade** — A row of arches supported on columns or piers, either
structural or purely decorative (blind arcading).

**Buttress** — A structure built against a wall to give it additional
support, resisting outward thrust. A **flying buttress** carries this
support across open space via an arch, rather than being solid masonry
built directly against the wall (Gothic).

**Capital** — The decorative top of a column, where it meets the arch or
beam above.

**Chevron** — A zigzag decorative pattern, common in Romanesque carving.

**Dome** — A rounded, usually hemispherical roof structure. In Byzantine
and Baroque architecture, often raised on a **drum** (a cylindrical wall
section) to increase height.

**Fanlight** — A semicircular or fan-shaped window above a doorway,
common in Georgian architecture.

**Gable** — The triangular section of wall at the end of a pitched roof,
between the sloping roof edges.

**Half-timbering** — Wall construction using an exposed timber frame with
infill panels between the beams. Structural in genuine medieval buildings;
almost always decorative-only in Tudor Revival work.

**Lancet window** — A tall, narrow window with a sharply pointed arch,
typical of Early English Gothic.

**Pediment** — A triangular (or sometimes curved) decorative element,
usually placed above a doorway or window, derived from classical Greek
temple architecture.

**Pendentive** — A curved, triangular section of masonry that allows a
round dome to sit on a square base, by smoothly transitioning the corners
of the square into the circular ring needed to support the dome
(Byzantine).

**Pilaster** — A flattened, decorative column attached to a wall surface,
not free-standing.

**Rib vault** — A ceiling structure where visible stone ribs form a
supporting skeleton, with lighter infill panels between them (Gothic).
**Fan vaulting** is a later, highly decorative English development where
the ribs spread out in a fan shape from each supporting point.

**Sash window** — A window with one or more panes that slide vertically
within the frame, typical of Georgian and later architecture.

**Tracery** — Decorative stone patterning that divides a Gothic window
into smaller sections, ranging from simple geometric shapes to elaborate
flowing designs depending on the period.

**Tympanum** — The semicircular or triangular decorative surface above a
doorway, often carved, common in both Romanesque and Gothic architecture.
